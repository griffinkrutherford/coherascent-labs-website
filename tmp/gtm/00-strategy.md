## 1. The honest read

You have over-invested in **assets** and under-invested in **distribution and instrumentation**.

What exists: 43 landing pages, 26 finished Meta ad creatives, 6 well-written blog posts, a
polished product site, a real app about to ship.

What does not exist: a single analytics pixel, a single social profile, a single automated
email, a sitemap, a robots.txt, a published price, or — most importantly — **one student who
has used the product**.

This is a common and fixable failure mode. The inventory is genuinely good; it is simply
pointed at nobody. Every recommendation in this plan follows from one instruction:

> **Stop building marketing assets. Start putting the product in human hands, this month.**

The window is unusually favorable. School starts now. The app ships in two weeks. The national
conversation about AI and cheating in school has never been louder, and you have built the
only product whose entire premise is that it *refuses to give the student the answer.* That is
a press story, a parent story, and a teacher story simultaneously. It is worth more than the
ad budget.

**Back-to-school is a five-week rolling wave, not a date.** TX and FL start ~Aug 12–13; NYC
starts **Sep 10**. A single national push wastes half the budget. Geo-phase it in three waves.

**The central trade:** you have hours and no dollars. Therefore the engine is direct human
outreach, community participation, and compounding SEO — not media buying. Paid spend exists
to answer the question *"which message makes a stranger stop?"* so the free channels can be
aimed at the winner.

## 3. Positioning

**Category:** Not a homework helper. Not an AI tutor. A **practice and feedback instrument**.

**One-line positioning:**
> Lune Synth reads the work you did by hand, finds the exact step where your reasoning broke,
> and builds practice around that one thing. It will not give you the answer.

**The wedge — and it is sharper than previously understood.** Competitive research confirms the
market splits on two axes: *what goes in* (the problem, or the student's own reasoning?) and
*what comes out* (the answer, or a diagnosis?).

Every competitor takes **the problem** as input. Photomath, Gauth, Symbolab, Mathway, Chegg
photograph *the textbook*. Khanmigo, MathGPT, Studdy take the problem *as text* and chat about
it. **Nobody photographs the student's handwritten work and localizes the error inside it.**

That produces the single strongest sentence available to this company:

> **The anti-cheating claim is enforced by the input format, not by a system prompt.**
> Every other "we don't give answers" product is one jailbreak away from giving answers.
> Lune Synth *cannot* give you the answer to a problem you haven't attempted — because your
> attempt is the input.

No competitor can copy that without rebuilding their product. Lead with it everywhere.

**Competitive notes that change tactics:**
- **The real incumbent is ChatGPT**, not Photomath — 54% of teens use it (Pew). Write against it.
- **The pricing anchor is Khanmigo at $4/mo**, not Photomath at $9.99. Any pricing conversation
  will hit "Khan Academy does Socratic for four dollars." The answer is not price — it is that
  *Khanmigo is a chatbot and cannot see your paper.*
- **MathGPT.ai already owns the phrase "cheat-proof"** but sells B2B at $25/student/course to
  college math departments. Do not fight for that phrase. Differentiate on consumer + mobile +
  handwriting + all subjects.
- **Chegg is down ~99% from peak with 45% layoffs.** That collapse is a free, citable, news-
  hooked attack line: the homework-answer business is dying, and it was never learning.

**Three audiences, three different sentences:**

| Audience | What they actually fear | The sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **Parents** (primary paid target) | My kid is outsourcing their brain and I can't tell | "You'll be able to see that they actually did the work." |
| **Adult learners** (nursing, pre-med, GED, career-change, grad exams) | I passed the practice test and still don't understand it | "Find out whether you missed the concept or just rushed." |
| **Teachers & tutors** (primary outreach target) | AI has made homework meaningless | "The one AI tool built to make students do more thinking, not less." |

**Voice discipline (do not violate).** Restrained, precise, respectful of the learner. Never
hypey, never fear-mongering at parents, never "crush your exams." The existing blog and landing
copy set the standard — match it. The brand's credibility *is* the product's credibility.

## 4. North star and the metrics that matter

**North star: Weekly Submitting Students (WSS)** — unique students who submitted at least one
handwritten attempt in the trailing 7 days.

Waitlist size is explicitly *not* a goal. It is a vanity number that has already grown without
producing a single user. WSS is the only figure that proves the core loop functions.

**The four numbers to review every Friday:**

| Metric | Why it's the one that matters | Phase-2 target |
|---|---|---|
| **WSS** | Proves the loop works | 100 by Sep 30 |
| **Second-attempt rate (7d)** | Did the feedback actually pull them back? This is the real product-market-fit signal. | ≥ 40% |
| **W4 retention** | Is this a habit or a novelty? | ≥ 25% |
| **Cost per activated student** | The only acquisition number worth tracking at this budget | < $15 |

**Counter-metrics to watch for self-deception:** waitlist signups without installs; installs
without submissions; impressions without clicks. Each of these can look like progress while
being none.

## 5. Funnel model (target: 100 activated students by Sep 30)

Working backward, with stated assumptions:

```
100 activated students (≥1 handwritten submission)
  ← ~300 TestFlight installs          [ASSUMPTION: 33% install→activation]
  ← ~1,000 interested/opted-in people [ASSUMPTION: 30% invite→install, high because opted-in]
```

**Where the ~1,000 come from — note how little of it is paid:**

| Source | Contribution | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Direct outreach (tutors, teachers, homeschool leaders, each bringing students) | ~300 | Hours only |
| Community participation (Reddit, Discord, FB groups) | ~200 | Hours only |
| Paid Meta testing (~$1,350 over 45 days @ ~$4 CPL) | ~340 | $1,350 [ASSUMPTION: CPL] |
| Warm network + referrals | ~100 | Hours only |
| Organic search + social + PR | ~150 | Hours only |

Roughly **80% of the target is reachable without media spend.** That is the plan's thesis in
one table. It is also why the outreach and content workstreams carry more weight here than paid.

## 6. Phases and gates

Do not begin a phase until the prior gate is met. Gates prevent scaling something broken.

### Phase 0 — "Instrument and arm" · Aug 3–16 (Weeks 1–2)
Nothing is measurable today. Fix that before spending a dollar or writing a campaign.
**Gate to Phase 1:** pixel + CAPI firing and verified · waitlist captures UTM, referrer, and
role · welcome and invite emails sending automatically · TestFlight external build approved by
Apple · sitemap.xml, robots.txt, and OG tags shipped · social handles claimed.

### Phase 1 — "First 100 real users" · Aug 17 – Sep 13 (Weeks 3–6)
Back-to-school. Get the product into hands and watch what happens. Paid runs its first single
creative test. Outreach runs at full volume. Publish the launch story.
**Gate to Phase 2:** 25+ activated students · ≥40% second-attempt rate · 3 written testimonials
· 5 design partners onboarded.

### Phase 2 — "Proof and compounding" · Sep 14 – Oct 25 (Weeks 7–12)
Fall test season. Turn early usage into evidence — case studies, real feedback screenshots,
retention data. Scale only what cleared the gate. PR push on the anti-cheating angle.
**Gate to Phase 3:** 100 WSS · W4 retention ≥25% · one validated paid creative under target CPL
· published pricing.

### Phase 3 — "Scale what proved out" · Oct 26 – Dec 20 (Weeks 13–20)
Public App Store launch, Product Hunt, earned media, and the January-semester run-up.

## 7. Standing risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Meta blocks targeting the actual end user.** Students under 18 cannot be targeted; ad accounts for youth-adjacent products get restricted. | High | Target parents (25–54) and adult learners only. Never imply the ad is for a minor. Parent landing pages already exist. |
| **$30/day never exits the learning phase**, producing noise instead of signal. | High | Treat paid as message-testing, not acquisition. One ad set at a time. Accept slow verdicts. |
| **App Store review delays** push the beta past back-to-school. | Medium | Every motion in Phases 0–1 must work without a shipped app. Waitlist and design-partner conversations proceed regardless. |
| **Handwriting recognition fails on real student work** in the wild. | Medium | This is why 25 users before 1,000. The gate exists to catch it. |
| **No published price** makes "50% off Pro" meaningless. | Certain (today) | Publish pricing before Phase 2. An unquantifiable discount is not an offer. |
| **Founder burns 40 hrs/wk on assets instead of humans.** | High — it is the demonstrated pattern | The weekly cadence blocks outreach time first. Assets get the remainder. |
| **TestFlight builds expire after 90 days.** An Aug 15 build dies ~**Nov 13** — precisely at the AP-order final deadline peak. Every tester silently loses access. | Certain if unmanaged | Calendar the re-upload for **Nov 1** now. Set a recurring 75-day rebuild reminder. |
| **TestFlight install friction** — six steps including installing an unfamiliar Apple app — silently kills non-technical parent and teacher conversions. | High | Record a 60-second install walkthrough video before the first invite. Send invites in batches of 50 so problems surface early. |

**Calibrated expectation on paid, so it isn't mistaken for failure:** at $30/day optimizing to a
$3–5 waitlist email, one valid creative read takes **10–14 days and $210–420**. That is 2–3
tests per month — roughly **6–9 tests for the entire fall**. Test radically different angles,
never 15% variants; at this volume you can only detect ~2× differences. Run **one campaign, one
ad set** — splitting the budget is the single most common micro-budget failure. And never adjust
budget by more than 20%, which resets the learning phase.

## 8. What to deliberately NOT do this quarter

Naming these prevents the plan from quietly expanding:

- **Do not build more landing pages.** 43 is more than enough for 0 users. The `/features/`
  pages in the existing expansion doc can wait until something is converting.
- **Do not produce more static subject ad creatives.** 26 exist and none have run.
- **Do not pursue school district or institutional sales.** Sales cycles of 6–18 months. Wrong
  motion for a pre-launch solo founder. Individual teachers, yes; procurement, no.
- **Do not launch on every social platform.** Pick the two that fit the format and go deep.
- **Do not build an Android version, a web app, or new features** in response to early feedback
  until the core loop retains.
- **Do not run paid to cold traffic at scale** before the second-attempt rate clears 40%.

## 9. Weekly operating rhythm

The same shape every week, so it survives busy weeks:

- **Every day** — Post the daily founder content piece (see workstream 05). Non-negotiable.
- **Every day** — Talk to at least one user or prospect. No exceptions.
- **Mon** — Review the four numbers. Set the week's single most important outcome.
- **Tue–Thu** — Outreach blocks first (mornings, before anything else). Content in afternoons.
- **Fri** — Ship the week's long-form content. Review paid. Write the weekly retro (3 lines:
  what moved, what didn't, what changes).
- **Sun** — Batch: film the week's short-form video, draft the week's daily posts.

The two daily items are the engine. Everything else is scheduling. If a week collapses, protect
the dailies and drop the rest.
