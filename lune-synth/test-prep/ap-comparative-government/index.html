Where Comp Gov points go missing

A regime type's definition is not a comparison between two countries.

The FRQs reward concept application, data analysis, and comparative argument across Britain, China, Iran, Mexico, Nigeria, and Russia. Students who know a concept well still lose points when they describe one country instead of comparing two.

The AP Comparative Government exam

Concept application and comparison across six countries.

The free-response section always asks you to apply concepts, read data, and compare across the course's six countries.

Multiple choice
55 questions in 1 hour 5 minutes. 50% of the score.
Free response
4 questions in 1 hour 30 minutes: concept application, quantitative analysis, comparative analysis, argument essay. 50% of the score.
Common weak spots
Describing one country where a comparison is required, and a regime classification with no stated reason.

A regime-classification habit

Name the type, then say what distinguishes it.

Your practice attempt

A regime that holds elections but limits press freedom is a hybrid regime.

What grading notices

The classification is right. The stronger answer also says what separates a hybrid regime from a full liberal democracy: elections exist, but civil liberties and judicial independence are constrained.

Luna's hint

What does a liberal democracy guarantee that a hybrid regime limits?
