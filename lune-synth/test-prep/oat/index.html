Physics is the difference

The OAT adds a full physics section, and optics is fair game.

Optometry applicants usually arrive strong in biology and chemistry. The physics section is where preparation gets uneven, and the errors are rarely about the concept. They come from signs: a reciprocal added instead of subtracted, a virtual image treated as real. Writing the setup turns a sign error into something feedback can point at.

The Optometry Admission Test

Four tests, scored from 200 to 400.

The OAT is a computer-based exam that covers the natural sciences, reading, physics and quantitative reasoning in a single appointment.

Survey of the Natural Sciences
100 questions in 90 minutes: biology, general chemistry and organic chemistry.
Reading Comprehension
50 questions in 60 minutes on scientific passages.
Physics and Quantitative Reasoning
40 physics questions in 50 minutes, then 40 quantitative reasoning questions in 45 minutes.
Common weak spots
Sign conventions in lens and mirror problems, and reading which step on an energy profile limits the rate.

Questions

OAT prep questions

Does Lune Synth cover OAT physics?

Yes. You can practise mechanics, optics, circuits and other introductory physics topics, and questions can include ray, lens and circuit diagrams.

Are these official OAT questions?

No. Lune Synth writes its own practice questions and is not affiliated with the Association of Schools and Colleges of Optometry or the OAT program.

How is the OAT different from the DAT?

Both share the natural sciences, reading and quantitative reasoning sections. The OAT has a physics section where the DAT has the Perceptual Ability Test.

Can I photograph my own work?

Yes. Scan handwritten work with your phone camera, or type or speak your reasoning instead.
