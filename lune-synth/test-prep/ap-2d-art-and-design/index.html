AP 2-D Art and Design with Lune Synth

Explain the color decision, not just make it.

AP 2-D Art and Design's portfolio rewards design decisions you can explain, such as which color scheme a piece uses and why, alongside the work itself. Practice naming your color and composition choices with feedback on your reasoning.

Where portfolio reasoning gets thin

Choosing colors by eye is fine; naming the scheme is the skill that transfers.

The Sustained Investigation and Selected Works sections ask you to articulate your process and materials. Being able to name a color scheme, a complement, or a value structure makes that written reflection specific instead of vague.

AP 2-D Art and Design

A portfolio, not a sit-down exam.

AP 2-D Art and Design is scored entirely on a submitted digital portfolio: no multiple choice and no timed free response.

Sustained Investigation
15 images documenting an inquiry-driven process, with written questions about materials and process. 60% of the score.
Selected Works
5 of your strongest works, each with a materials and process statement. 40% of the score.
What is scored
Range of approaches, mastery of materials and elements like color, and the depth of investigation across the portfolio.

Questions shown are practice questions written for Lune Synth and rendered in the app, not official AP items. Exam formats are summarised from the College Board's course and exam descriptions; check AP Central for current details.

A color-vocabulary habit

Name the scheme precisely, not approximately.

Your practice attempt

Blue, yellow-orange, and red-orange look balanced together.

What grading notices

They do work together, and there's a name for why: blue's complement is orange, and yellow-orange and red-orange sit on either side of it, which makes this a split-complementary scheme.

Luna's hint

What color is directly opposite blue on the wheel?

Targeted next practice

Three color-wheel questions asking you to name the scheme a set of marked hues forms.
