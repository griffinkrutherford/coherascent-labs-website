AP Latin with Lune Synth

Get the case ending right, then the translation follows.

AP Latin free response rewards translating Vergil and Caesar accurately, which depends on recognizing a noun's case from its ending, not guessing from word order. Practice declensions and translation with feedback on your reasoning.

Where Latin points go missing

A good guess at meaning is not the same as reading the case.

Translation and analytical essays lose points when a case ending is misread, which changes a word's grammatical role (and the sentence's meaning) even if the vocabulary is known.

The AP Latin exam

Translation and analysis of the required Vergil and Caesar readings.

The exam tests close reading of the specific Vergil and Caesar passages on the required reading list, combining multiple choice with translation and analytical essays.

Multiple choice
Reading comprehension and grammatical analysis of Latin passages, about 49% of the score.
Free response
A sight translation, a translation of a studied passage, and analytical essays, about 49% of the score.
Course Project
Two in-class checkpoint tasks scored by your teacher during the year, about 2% of the score.
Common weak spots
Misreading a case ending and guessing a word's role from its position instead, and analytical essays with no cited Latin as evidence.

What grading notices

Rēgis is the genitive singular. Third-declension ablative singulars usually end in -e, which gives rēge here.
