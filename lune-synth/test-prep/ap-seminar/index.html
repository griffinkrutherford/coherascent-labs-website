AP Seminar with Lune Synth

Connect the evidence to the claim, explicitly.

AP Seminar's end-of-course exam and team project both score how well you connect evidence to a claim with stated reasoning, not just whether the evidence is relevant. Practice that connective step with feedback on your argument.

Where Seminar points go missing

Relevant evidence with no reasoning is half an argument.

The course and its exam reward explicitly stating why evidence supports a claim, which source qualifies as a perspective, and where an argument's reasoning is weak, not just gathering good sources.

AP Seminar

A team project and an individual essay, then a written end-of-course exam.

AP Seminar combines a team research project, an individual research essay with its own presentation and defense, and a single written end-of-course exam.

Team Project and Presentation
A 1,200-word team research report plus an 8-10 minute team presentation and defense. 20% of the score.
Individual Research, Presentation, and Defense
A 2,000-word written argument, a 6-8 minute presentation, and an oral defense answering questions from your teacher. 35% of the score.
End-of-course exam
A 2-hour written exam: short-answer analysis of a source set, then an essay building and supporting an argument. 45% of the score.

Questions shown are practice questions written for Lune Synth and rendered in the app, not official AP items. Exam formats are summarised from the College Board's course and exam descriptions; check AP Central for current details.

A reasoning habit

Say what the evidence implies for the claim.

Your practice attempt

The survey shows students are tired, so start times should change.

What grading notices

The survey is cited. The reasoning step is missing: the response needs to say why less sleep is a health problem a later start time would fix.

Luna's hint

What does the survey's finding about sleep imply about student health?

Targeted next practice
