Judgment is the tested skill

Knowing the content is not the same as choosing what to do first.

NCLEX items give you a client, a set of cues and several actions that are all reasonable on their own. The exam rewards the order: which cue matters most, what it could mean, and which action addresses it safely. When you write that chain out, a wrong priority stops looking like bad luck and starts looking like a specific, fixable step.

The Next Generation NCLEX

An adaptive exam built around clinical judgment.

Since April 2023 the NCLEX has used the Next Generation format: case studies that unfold over several questions, plus new item types that ask you to sort, match and prioritize rather than pick one option.

Format
Computerized adaptive testing: the exam picks each item from your earlier answers and stops once a pass or fail decision is clear.
Length
NCLEX-RN runs from 85 to 150 items with up to five hours of testing time, including unscored pretest items.
Item types
Unfolding case studies and stand-alone items: multiple response, drag and drop, drop-down cloze, matrix, highlight and bow-tie.
Common weak spots
Choosing a comfort or teaching action ahead of an airway, breathing or circulation problem, and dosage setups that skip a unit conversion.

Questions

NCLEX prep questions

Does Lune Synth use real NCLEX questions?

No. Lune Synth generates its own practice questions and grades your written or spoken reasoning. It is not affiliated with NCSBN, and its questions are not official NCLEX items.

Can it help with dosage calculations?

Yes. Questions can show a dose card with the order, the client's weight and the available concentration. You write the setup with units and feedback points at the factor that went wrong.

Does it cover the Next Generation item types?

Lune Synth practises the reasoning those items test: recognizing cues, prioritizing and justifying actions. It does not reproduce the NCLEX testing interface, so use NCSBN's own materials to get used to the item formats.

Is this useful for the NCLEX-PN as well as the NCLEX-RN?

The habit is the same for both: say what you noticed, what it means and what you would do first. You set the topic and level for each practice set.
