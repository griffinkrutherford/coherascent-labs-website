The AP Music Theory exam

Written theory, aural skills, and sight-singing together.

The exam mixes multiple choice (including listening) with written free response covering dictation, part-writing, and analysis, plus a sight-singing component.

Multiple choice
About 1 hour 20 minutes, including questions answered from recorded musical excerpts.
Free response
About 1 hour 10 minutes: melodic and harmonic dictation, part-writing from a figured bass or Roman-numeral progression, and melody harmonization.
Sight-singing
A separately timed section, about 10 minutes, where you sing and record two notated melodies on your own.
Common weak spots
Counting scale degrees from the wrong key signature, and inversions mislabeled from the bass note.

Questions shown are practice questions written for Lune Synth and rendered in the app, not official AP items. Exam formats are summarised from the College Board's course and exam descriptions; check AP Central for current details.

What grading notices

C-Eb-G isn't built from B-flat major's fourth scale degree. Counting up from B-flat, C is the second scale degree, which makes the chord ii.
