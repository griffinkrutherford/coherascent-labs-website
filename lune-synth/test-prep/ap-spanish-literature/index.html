AP Spanish Literature and Culture with Lune Synth

Nombra el recurso, luego explica su efecto.

AP Spanish Literature free response scores whether you can name a specific literary device and explain its effect in the text, not just discuss the poem generally. Practice that close reading with feedback on your analysis.

Where Spanish Literature points go missing

Describing a verse is not the same as naming its recurso.

Analysis questions reward identifying the specific device, such as hipérbaton or anáfora, and explaining what it does to meaning or rhythm, not a paraphrase of the verse.

The AP Spanish Literature and Culture exam

Close reading of a required list of texts, in Spanish.

The exam is entirely in Spanish and draws on a required reading list spanning medieval to contemporary texts, combining multiple choice with textual analysis essays.

Multiple choice
Reading comprehension and analysis of the required texts, about 50% of the score.
Free response
Short-answer and essay analysis of a single text and a comparison of two texts, about 50% of the score.
Common weak spots
Paraphrasing a verse instead of naming its device, and essays that describe a text without textual evidence.

Questions shown are practice questions written for Lune Synth and rendered in the app, not official AP items. Exam formats are summarised from the College Board's course and exam descriptions; check AP Central for current details.
