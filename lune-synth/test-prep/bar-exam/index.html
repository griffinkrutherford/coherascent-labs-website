Rules are not the hard part

Most missed points come from applying a rule you already know.

Bar candidates finish months of outlines and still miss questions where they could recite the rule. The miss usually sits in the application: an element treated as satisfied without a fact behind it, an exception that was never checked, or a conclusion that answers a different issue. The bar exam

Multiple choice, essays and a performance test.

Most jurisdictions use the Uniform Bar Examination, built from three NCBE components. NCBE began introducing its NextGen bar exam in July 2026, and jurisdictions are switching on different timelines, so confirm which version you will sit.

MBE
200 multiple-choice questions over two three-hour sessions: Civil Procedure, Constitutional Law, Contracts, Criminal Law and Procedure, Evidence, Real Property and Torts.
MEE
Six 30-minute essays that ask you to identify the issues and apply the law to a fact pattern.
MPT
Two 90-minute performance tasks: a lawyering assignment completed from a supplied file and library.
Common weak spots
Conclusions stated without the fact that satisfies each element, and exceptions that are never tested against the facts.

Questions

Bar exam prep questions

Does Lune Synth use real MBE questions?

No. Lune Synth writes its own practice questions and is not affiliated with NCBE or any bar examiner. For licensed past questions, use NCBE's study aids alongside it.

Can it help with essays?

It can give feedback on short written analyses: whether you named the rule, applied it to the facts and reached a conclusion that follows. It does not grade full-length timed essays the way a bar examiner would.

Does it cover the MPRE?

The MPRE is a separate professional responsibility exam that most jurisdictions also require. You can set professional responsibility as a topic and practise applying the rules to short fact patterns the same way.

Will this work for the NextGen bar exam?

The core skill carries over: reading facts, identifying the issue and applying the law. Check NCBE and your jurisdiction for which exam version you will take and its format.
