Lune Synth for Microbiology

Read the gel, map the plasmid, explain the growth curve.

Microbiology asks you to move between a bacterial culture and the molecular tools used to study it: a restriction digest run on a gel, a plasmid map with cut sites, a coding strand to translate. Lune Synth sets up the gel or the map with a gap and checks your reasoning about fragment sizes and cut positions, not just the vocabulary.

Where microbiology students get stuck

Knowing what a restriction enzyme does is not the same as reading its gel.

Students can define a restriction enzyme and still misread a gel: which lane is the single digest, which band pair should sum to the plasmid length, where the double digest should land. Working the fragment math is the skill the lab practical and the exam both test.
