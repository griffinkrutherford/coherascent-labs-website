Lune Synth for Spanish

Pick the tense, conjugate the verb, say what you mean.

Spanish gets hard right where English stops helping: preterite or imperfect, indicative or subjunctive, ser or estar, and the agreement that follows each choice. Lune Synth gives you conversations with a missing line and conjugation tables with a blank to fill, then points to the form that went wrong and why.

Where Spanish learners get stuck

You recognise the right form. You can't produce it on demand.

Multiple-choice apps train recognition: you can spot comió in a lineup and still write comía in a sentence that needs the preterite. The gap shows up when you speak or write, because nobody hands you four options. Building the form yourself, and knowing which rule picked it, is what closes that gap.

Practice that looks like the language

Conversations, conjugation tables and picture cards.

Questions can carry a short conversation with one line or one verb missing, a conjugation table with the asked form left blank, or picture cards for new vocabulary with the article shown. The missing piece stays a question mark, so the figure sets up the problem without giving the answer away.

Captured from the Lune Synth app. Generated question sets can include a diagram when one helps; figures go through automated checks against the question before they are shown. Tables for regular verbs are recomputed by the app's own conjugation model, and a table with a form that disagrees is dropped; irregular verbs are checked for structure only. Picture cards draw every word from a reviewed vocabulary list, not from the model.

The Lune Synth loop

The student thinks first. The technology helps second.

01

Produce the form

Write on paper or the in-app canvas, type, or answer by voice. Dictation is transcribed and graded as text; Lune Synth does not score your pronunciation.

02

See which rule broke

Feedback separates the wrong mood from the wrong person, a missed spelling change (llegues, not llegas) from a missed accent.

03

Drill the pattern you missed

A quick mission goes after that pattern: subjunctive triggers, preterite versus imperfect, or gender and agreement.
