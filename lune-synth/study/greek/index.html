Lune Synth for Greek

Decline the article, pick the aspect, read the alphabet.

Modern Greek keeps a case system like Latin's, nominative, genitive, accusative and vocative, carried by the article as much as the noun. Then it layers on something Latin doesn't ask for: most verbs have two stems, one for an ongoing or repeated action and one for a single completed action, so "I was writing" and "I wrote" aren't just different endings, they're different words. Lune Synth gives you article-and-noun declension tables and aspect-pair tables with one stem missing.

Where Greek learners get stuck

Two different pasts, and no English rule tells you which.

έγραφα (I was writing, imperfective) and έγραψα (I wrote, perfective) both translate as a simple English past tense, so a learner reading a dialogue can glide past the distinction entirely. Choosing between the two stems only becomes automatic once you've had to build each one yourself, in a sentence that forces the choice.
