Lune Synth for English learners

Phrasal verbs, vowel sounds and everyday words, in context.

English is full of small words that change everything: run into, run out of, run over. Its spelling hides its sounds, so ship and sheep look closer than they sound. Lune Synth gives you short dialogues with one word missing, phonemic transcriptions that make the vowels visible, and picture cards for everyday vocabulary.

Where English learners get stuck

Correct grammar, wrong small word.

Intermediate learners often know the rules and still choose run onto, depend of or listen the radio, because the small words are learned one at a time, in context. Seeing them in real exchanges and supplying them yourself builds the instinct that rules alone do not.

Practice that looks like the language

Dialogues, transcriptions and vocabulary cards.

Questions can carry a short dialogue with one word missing, a list of words with their General American phonemic transcriptions and one to supply, or picture cards for everyday vocabulary with one word left blank.

Captured from the Lune Synth app. Generated question sets can include a diagram when one helps; figures go through automated checks against the question before they are shown. Transcriptions are checked against the IPA and against the English phoneme set for the variety shown. Picture cards draw every word from a reviewed vocabulary list, not from the model.

The Lune Synth loop

The student thinks first. The technology helps second.

01

Use the word

Write on paper or the in-app canvas, type, or answer by voice. Dictation is transcribed and graded as text; Lune Synth does not score your pronunciation.

02

See what slipped

Feedback separates a wrong particle from a wrong verb, and a vowel you misread from a word you didn't know.

03

A quick mission targets it: phrasal verbs with run, get and take, or long and short vowel pairs.
