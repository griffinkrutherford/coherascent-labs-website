Lune Synth for Arabic

Find the root, build the pattern, read right to left.

Arabic hides most of its vocabulary inside roots: د ر س sits under دَرَسَ (he studied), دَرَّسَ (he taught), مَدْرَسَة (school) and دِراسَة (study), and a learner who only recognises each word separately never sees the shape underneath. Lune Synth gives you right-to-left dialogues with translations and one line missing, and root-and-pattern tables where you name the three letters a family of words shares, then feedback on the exact letter or pattern that went wrong.

Where Arabic learners get stuck

Every word has a root. Spotting it isn't the same as finding it.

An app that drills كتب as one flashcard never asks you to see that كاتب (writer), مكتوب (written) and مكتبة (library) all stand on the same three letters in a different pattern. Reading diacritics that a native text never prints, or tapping a translation you half-recognise, doesn't build that skill. Pulling the root out of a word you've never seen, and predicting the pattern of a word you need, does.

Practice that reads the way Arabic reads

Right-to-left dialogues and root-and-pattern tables.

Questions can carry a short dialogue in Arabic script, laid out right to left with an English translation under each turn and one reply missing, or a root-and-pattern table that lines up a verb's forms and its derived nouns so you can name the three letters they all share. The asked piece stays a question mark, never pre-filled.

Captured from the Lune Synth app. Generated question sets can include a diagram when one helps; figures go through automated checks against the question before they are shown. Root-and-pattern tables are checked for structure (the asked root not printed elsewhere in the table), not recomputed by a morphology model. Lune Synth does not grade pronunciation or short-vowel diacritics you choose not to write.
