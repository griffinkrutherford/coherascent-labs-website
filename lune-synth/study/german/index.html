Lune Synth for German

Choose the case, then the ending. Know why.

German asks three questions before every article: which gender, which case, which number. Lune Synth gives you article and case tables with one ending to supply, conversations where a preposition decides between dative and accusative, and picture cards that show each noun with der, die or das.

Where German learners get stuck

Memorising the table is not the same as using it.

Most learners can recite der, den, dem, des and still write mit den Freund, because the hard part is not the table: it is deciding which row the sentence needs. Wo or wohin, subject or object, a preposition that always takes the dative. Practise the decision, and the ending follows.

Practice that looks like the language

Case tables, conversations and der, die, das.

Questions can carry an article or conjugation table with the asked cell left blank, a short dialogue where one article depends on the preposition, or picture cards with each noun's gender shown. The missing piece stays a question mark until you supply it.

Captured from the Lune Synth app. Generated question sets can include a diagram when one helps; figures go through automated checks against the question before they are shown. Article tables and tables for regular (weak) verbs are recomputed by the app's own model, and a table that disagrees is dropped; strong and irregular verbs are checked for structure only. Picture cards draw every word from a reviewed vocabulary list, not from the model.

The Lune Synth loop

The student thinks first. The technology helps second.

01

Build the ending

Write on paper or the in-app canvas, type, or answer by voice. Dictation is transcribed and graded as text; Lune Synth does not score your pronunciation.

02

See which choice broke

Feedback separates a wrong gender from a wrong case, and a wrong case from a word-order slip.

03

Repeat the decision

A quick mission drills that decision: two-way prepositions, dative verbs, or accusative masculine endings.

Student attempt

Ich stelle die Tasche auf dem Stuhl.

What grading notices

Stuhl is masculine and the article form exists, but stellen describes movement onto the chair, so auf takes the accusative here.

Luna's hint

Is the bag already on the chair, or moving onto it: wo or wohin?
