Lune Synth for Korean

Build the block, then choose how politely to say it.

Hangul is logical once you see how each block is built, and Korean grammar asks a second question in every sentence: who are you talking to? Lune Synth shows syllable blocks as jamo tiles with one block to build, tables of plain and polite verb forms, and conversations where the reply has to match the listener.

Where Korean learners get stuck

The words are right. The speech level is wrong.

Learners often stop at reading Hangul by sound and memorising one verb ending, then use 반말 with a teacher or a stiff form with a friend. The fix is practising the choice: the same sentence, a different listener, a different ending. Building blocks and endings yourself makes both automatic.

The Lune Synth loop

The student thinks first. The technology helps second.

01

Answer in Korean

Write Hangul by hand on paper or the in-app canvas, or type. Answers are graded as text; Lune Synth does not score pronunciation.

02

See which choice broke

Feedback separates a wrong tense from a wrong speech level, and a wrong jamo from a wrong block.

03

Drill that choice

A quick mission targets it: polite past endings, 반말 versus 존댓말, or blocks with final consonants.
