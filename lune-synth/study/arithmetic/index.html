Lune Synth for Arithmetic

See the fraction, write the method, fix the one step that slipped.

For a child learning fractions or long multiplication, or an adult rebuilding number sense, a red X on a wrong answer says nothing about what went wrong. Lune Synth shows fraction bars, tape diagrams and column layouts, reads the written method, and names the single step to repair.

Where arithmetic learners get stuck

A small slip should not feel like the whole method failed.

Most arithmetic errors are local: a forgotten carry, a misaligned column, comparing fractions by their denominators alone. When the only feedback is a wrong final answer, learners conclude they are bad at math. Pointing at the one step keeps the rest of the method, and the confidence, intact.

Models before rules

Fraction bars, tape diagrams and column methods.

Questions can include the visual models used in classrooms: fraction bars of the same size, a tape split into equal groups, or a multiplication laid out with boxes for each carry. The model makes the quantity visible; the learner still does the working.

Captured from the Lune Synth app. Generated question sets can include a diagram when one helps; figures go through automated checks against the question before they are shown.
