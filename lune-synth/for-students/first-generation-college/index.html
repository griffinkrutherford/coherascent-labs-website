For first-generation college students

Check your work at midnight, not just in office hours.

When you're the first in your family at college, there may be no one at home to ask whether your setup is right. Lune Synth checks the work you wrote, step by step, and Luna answers questions about it with hints, not answers. Office hours are still worth going to, and you'll arrive knowing exactly what to ask.

The unwritten rules

Nobody tells you how to study for a college exam.

Plenty of students learn from family how college works: that rereading isn't studying, that problem sets are practice for the exam, and that you can ask a professor about one wrong step. If you're working that out yourself, Lune Synth covers the practice side: work problems, see which step went wrong, and practise that step.

The question in the screenshot

Example: checking one step of an equation

Your work

“3x + 5 = 20, so 3x = 20 + 5 = 25, and x = 25/3.”

What feedback points to

Getting the x term on its own first is the right plan. To undo + 5, subtract 5 from both sides: 3x = 20 − 5 = 15, so x = 5. Adding 5 moved the equation the wrong way.

Luna's hint

What undoes adding 5? Do that to both sides.

Questions

Questions from first-generation students

Does this replace tutoring or office hours?

No. Professors, TAs and campus tutoring centres know your course in ways an app can't. Lune Synth fills the hours in between with practice and feedback, so the help you get in person goes further.

Will Luna just give me the answer?

No. Luna gives hints, key terms, diagrams and questions that build on your own attempt, and won't hand over a copyable solution. You finish the step yourself.

Can I use my own course materials?

Yes. Constellations can be built from lecture PDFs, homework, a syllabus, a study guide, photos of work on paper or a YouTube link. Problem Solver works through a problem you bring in, step by step.
