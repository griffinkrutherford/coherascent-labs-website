For people teaching themselves

You found the lectures. Now get the feedback.

Free courses, textbooks and video lectures give self-learners plenty to read and watch. What's usually missing is someone checking your work. Lune Synth asks you to solve and explain, checks each step, and gives you practice on whatever didn't hold, from dynamic programming to differential equations.

The self-study trap

Following along feels like understanding.

A video walkthrough is easy to follow and hard to reproduce. Self-taught learners rarely find out which parts they can't do alone, because nobody asks them to. Lune Synth asks: every question needs your attempt before you see any feedback.

Your work

“Memoization stores answers so you don't recompute them. Tabulation is the same thing, but with a table.”

What feedback points to

Storing subproblem answers is the shared idea, and that part is right. The difference is direction. Memoization is top-down: start from the full problem, recurse and cache results. Tabulation is bottom-up: fill the table from the smallest subproblems first.

Questions

Questions from self-taught learners

Do I need a class or a teacher to use it?

No. Lune Synth works on its own: type a topic or add your materials, practise, and get feedback on your work.

What can I teach myself with it?

Anything with problems or explanations to practise: math, computer science, the sciences, economics, history, writing, languages and more, from beginner to graduate level.

How does it know whether my answer is right?

Feedback is checked against a rubric for the question, step by step. You can inspect the feedback and challenge a step you think was marked wrong.
