For people switching careers

Learn your next field's fundamentals, not just its vocabulary.

A new career often starts with a course: accounting, statistics, coding or a healthcare prerequisite. The lectures make it look easy; the problems are harder. Lune Synth builds practice from the materials you're already using, checks the work you write, and turns weak spots into short missions you can do after work.

Watching isn't the same as doing

A course you've watched isn't a skill you can use yet.

Online courses and certificate programs are full of lectures that make sense while you watch. The real test is the first problem you do alone, like a journal entry or a confidence interval. Lune Synth puts that at the centre: you work the problem, and feedback shows exactly where it went wrong.

An example from accounting

Example feedback on an accrued expense

Your work

December wages of $3,000 are paid on January 5. December entry: “Debit Cash $3,000, Credit Wages Expense $3,000.”

What feedback points to

Recognising that the wages belong in December is the key idea, and it's right. No cash was paid in December, so Cash doesn't move yet. The December entry is Debit Wages Expense $3,000, Credit Wages Payable $3,000.

Luna's hint

Did any cash leave the business in December? If not, which account records an amount you owe?

Next practice

Four accrual and deferral entries, then a short set on the January payment that clears the payable.

Questions

Questions from career changers

Which fields does Lune Synth cover?

Fields with problems to practise: accounting, finance, statistics, computer science, nursing prerequisites such as anatomy and dosage calculations, writing and more. Type a topic or add your course materials to start.

Can it replace my course or certificate program?

No. Lune Synth isn't a credential and doesn't replace your program. It's where you practise what the program teaches and get feedback on your work.

What can I use to build practice?

Constellations can be built from YouTube links, lecture PDFs, homework, a syllabus, a study guide, photos of work on paper or typed notes.

I haven't done math in years. Where do I start?

Start with the fundamentals your new field uses. If the math itself is rusty, the adult learners page covers getting it back.
