If your notes pile up unread

Take notes. Then close them and test yourself.

Notes help most when you use them to practise. Lune Synth has a handwriting notebook with pens, paper styles, pages, folders and audio recordings. When you're ready, close the notes and practise what's in them, with feedback on what you could recall and what you couldn't.

Why rereading notes feels better than it works

Rereading notes builds familiarity, not recall.

Your notes look right because you wrote them. That's recognition. In one well-known experiment, students who practised recalling a passage remembered more of it a week later than students who restudied it (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006). On an exam you have to produce the idea with the notes closed, so that's how to practise.

Example: recalling a page on the cell cycle

From memory

“Mitosis has four phases: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase. The DNA is copied in prophase.”

What feedback points to

The four phases, in the right order, are correct. The DNA is copied earlier, in the S phase of interphase, before mitosis starts. Prophase is when the copied chromosomes condense.

Luna's hint

Mitosis begins with two copies of every chromosome already there. When in the cycle were they made?

Questions

Questions about notes in Lune Synth

What can the notebook do?

Write and draw with pen and highlighter presets, choose a paper style, add pages, type text boxes, and select and move ink with the lasso. Notes live in folders with search and pinning, and deleted notes go to Recently Deleted first so you can restore them.

Can I record a lecture into a note?

Yes. A note can hold audio recordings, each with a transcript you can edit, alongside your handwritten pages.

Can I export my notes?

Yes. Export a whole note as a PDF, or the current page as a PNG, through your device's share sheet.

How do notes become practice?

Add your notes to a Constellation as photos, a PDF export or typed text, alongside lecture PDFs or a study guide, and Lune Synth builds connected skill missions from them. The blog post Close the notes. Find the gaps. explains why this works better than rereading.
