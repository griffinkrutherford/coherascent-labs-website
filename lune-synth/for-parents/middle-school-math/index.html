For parents of middle schoolers

Catch the fraction gap before algebra builds on it.

Middle school math moves from fractions and ratios to equations quickly. A gap in one shows up later in the next. Lune Synth asks questions with the fraction bars and tape diagrams students use in class, reads your child's written work, and practises the specific gap it finds.

Why middle school is the moment

Algebra builds on fractions and ratios.

A child who divides fractions by rote, or isn't sure what a ratio means, can keep up for a while and then struggle when algebra arrives. Lune Synth asks for the reasoning, such as explaining a division with the bar. That shows whether your child understands the idea or only remembers a rule.

The question in the screenshot

Example feedback on 2/3 ÷ 1/6

Your child's work

“2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 2/18 = 1/9 scoop.” The fractions were multiplied instead of divided.

What feedback points to

The division is set up correctly, but line 2 multiplies the fractions. An answer smaller than one scoop can't be right for 2/3 cup.

Luna's hint

Split each third on the bar into two equal pieces. How many sixths are shaded?
