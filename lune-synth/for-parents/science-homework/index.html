For parents of teens taking science

Science homework where the diagram is half the question.

A circuit, a Punnett square, a phase diagram: in science homework the figure carries half the information, and if you haven't read one in years it's hard to check. Lune Synth draws the figure into the question, reads your teen's working, units and all, and points to the step where the reasoning slipped.

Why science homework stalls

Most mistakes happen before the arithmetic.

A teen who mixes up series and parallel, or reads a phase diagram's axes the wrong way round, can do every calculation correctly and still get the question wrong. An answer key only shows the final number. Lune Synth looks at the setup: which resistors share current, which alleles each parent passes on, which region of the diagram a point sits in.

Example feedback on the circuit

Your teen's work

“Total R = 4 + 6 + 3 = 13 Ω, so I = 12/13 A and V1 = 4 × 12/13 ≈ 3.7 V.” All three resistors were added as if they were in series.

What feedback points to

Using V = IR across R1 is the right plan. The 6 Ω and 3 Ω resistors are in parallel, so together they make 2 Ω, not 9 Ω, and that changes the current through R1.

Luna's hint

When two resistors sit side by side, is their combined resistance bigger or smaller than either one alone?

Questions parents ask

Before you sign up

Which sciences does it cover?

Physics, chemistry and biology, plus related subjects such as anatomy and physiology, from introductory courses through AP level. Generated question sets can include a diagram when one helps.

Can my teen practise from their teacher's notes?

Yes. When creating a Quick Mission, your teen can describe the unit and attach files such as class notes or a worksheet, and the practice set is built around them.

Will it just give my teen the answer?

No. Feedback points to the step that went wrong, and Luna, the in-app tutor, gives hints and questions rather than a finished solution.

Is Lune Synth for my teen's age?

During the beta, Lune Synth accounts are for learners 13 and older, and a teen who is not yet an adult where you live uses it with a parent's or guardian's permission. Children under 13 can't create an account or join the waitlist on their own. The Terms and Privacy Policy have the details.
