For parents of kids who say they're bad at math

Find the exact step where your child's math went wrong.

When your child says they're bad at math, the trouble is often a single step. Lune Synth reads the handwritten page, points to the line where the work went off track, and gives a hint toward the next move. It doesn't hand over the answer.

When ‘I don't get it’ is all you hear

One wrong step can feel like ‘I'm just bad at math.’

A wrong final answer doesn't tell you much. Your child might have the right idea and lose a sign, or apply a rule backwards on one line. Lune Synth starts from the work on the page. It marks what was right and narrows the problem to one step they can fix.

“Wrong. Try again.”
→
“Line 2: the 3 should come off both sides.”

What your child does, and what Lune Synth does

Your child does the math. Lune Synth points to the step.

01

Pictures that match the method

Questions can come with the diagram classrooms use, such as a bar model for 2x + 3 = 11. That gives your child something concrete to reason from, not a bare equation.

02

Feedback on each step

Feedback goes through the work step by step. It notes what held up and names the first line that needs fixing.

03

A hint, not the answer

Luna, the in-app tutor, asks a question about that step. Your child still writes the fix.

The question in the screenshot

Example feedback on 2x + 3 = 11

Your child's work

2x + 3 = 11 → 2x = 14 → x = 7. The setup is right, but the 3 went the wrong way on line 2.

What feedback points to

Writing the equation was correct. Line 2 added 3 to both sides instead of subtracting it, so every line after that is off.

Luna's hint

Look at the bar. If the 3 is part of the 11, how much is left for the two x pieces?
